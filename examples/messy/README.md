# A messy data map

Brindlewick, a made-up online shop, the way its data map looked before its first review. The date of birth has no
lawful basis and a retention that names only an event, the sick notes are health data with no Article 9 condition, the
face photo is kept forever, the call notes have no purpose and are stored in a country written as Germny, the phone
number goes to a call centre in India with no safeguard and has no owner, the customer id rests on goodwill, the device
id goes to Singapore under the withdrawn Privacy Shield, the marketing opt-in goes to the United States on an adequacy
decision and names no consent reference, the card token goes to a payment processor with no agreement and is listed
twice, the background check is criminal offence data, the IP address has no category and no storage location, the gift
note address goes to Morocco on a derogation, and the last row has three fields where the header has seventeen. The
notes column is not a column the tool reads.

It also holds what must not be reported: the email goes to the United States under SCCs, the delivery address has its
basis written as Art. 6(1)(b) and a retention that says permanently next to a period, the union dues are special
category data with their Article 9 condition, the background check goes to the United Kingdom on an adequacy decision,
the sick notes are kept internally, the device id is stored in a cloud region that `examples/config.json` names as
Germany, and the interests of the leads rest on consent with the consent reference written down.
